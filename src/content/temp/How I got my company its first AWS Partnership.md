I was operating comfortably at team level — being a leader for my team, helping them navigate tech, people, and processes — and was enjoying what I did.

But I always wanted to make an impact beyond my team. I knew I had lots to offer beyond a team but I did not know how. I asked Anand about any problem the company is looking to solve that I can lead. Something that is challenging enough for me and where I can deploy my skills effectively. Many ideas were discussed, but not many were aligned with my skills or that I could fully own.

## The ambition
I never stopped poking Anand about it and then one day, during one of those brainstorming conversations, Anand mentioned that Technogise is looking to become an AWS Partner to help boost its consulting position and to help with sales pipeline and that he felt that is one program that is independent and where I can help.

It aligned with my own goal to do something beyond teams and making an impact across the organisation.

I had no idea what it took for a company to become an AWS Partner but I got interested and I signed up for it.

At this time, Anand did not know about the program any more than I did, except that he wanted Technogise to become an AWS partner. We just candidly had this conversation and dispersed.

## The Initial Exploration
For the next few days, I googled about AWS partnership, what it is, what it requires from companies, how to get there, and so on.

After a few days of exploration, I understood that AWS offers 3 tiers of partnership to companies and that the companies have to meet certain requirements and pay an annual fee to be eligible for a given tier.

However, I did not know how a company would get to a tier and how AWS would know if the company meets its partnership requirements.

The only other resource I had was an AWS Partner Central account that one of my colleagues — Vivek Veeramani — had created; who was also exploring himself, because no one knew what to do.

I had no idea what a Partner Central was but I was hopeful by exploring the portal, I may find some answers or may be a direction. So I asked for the credentials for the portal and looked around.

## The Feedback Loop
After spending a few weeks online and on the Partner Central, I understood some more about the different tiers — Select, Advanced, and Premier — and their requirements.

However, I still did not have a full understanding of those requirements and if what I had gathered so far was enough to report back to Anand.

So I started putting together what I learned so far in a Google doc and shared it with Anand and Vivek Veeramani. I scheduled a call to go over my findings with them.

I explained to them that I did not have a concrete plan, but just that there are tiers and each tier has a requirement to be met.

The call was more of a progress report than a project plan, but I felt summarising what's done, what's next, and the challenges I'm facing is important.

I made sure I reported the progress every few weeks to them. A habit I followed throughout the program. Sometimes, it was formal, most of the times, it was over Slack — not in a formal format, but just enough information that answers Anand's question since the last time we met and my plan for the next steps.

## The Starting Point
By the end of about a month and a half, I understood that each partner had to choose a **Partner Path** based on their business model and each tier — **Select, Advanced, and Premier** — had their own requirements to be met as below.

1. **AWS Accredited Individuals:** X employees who had to be accredited by AWS
2. **AWS Certified Individuals:** X employees who had to be AWS certified
3. **Launched Opportunities:** Demonstrate opportunities that were launched by the company
4. **Monthly Recurring Revenue Minimum (MRRM):** A minimum monthly recurring revenue in $ from those opportunities

I went to Anand and Vivek with this information and did the following things:

1. Finalised that we'd fall in the **Services** Path based on our business model
2. Finalised that since we're at zero, we'd target to be in the **Select tier** first as it makes the most sense
3. One thing was certain that we had to get people in our company **AWS certified**

I had previous experience being AWS certified myself, so I was comfortable guiding others how to choose a certification, how to prepare for it, how to book the exam, and so on.

I told Anand that this is an independent track and that it is the most clear direction at this point for a concrete plan, so while Vivek Veeramani took the ownership of finding how to get individuals AWS accredited and what exactly the Launched Opportunities and MRRM meant, I started documenting information about the AWS certifications — foundational, associate, professional, speciality, and so on — so I can set the expectations with Anand accordingly.

By this time, I became the admin for the Partner portal and started managing users. I created accounts for Anand, Vivek Veeramani, Vivek Singh, and this activity created a bunch of tasks on the portal automatically for us that we had to finish in order to complete the whole onboarding journey. These tasks included things such as having an AWS marketplace, paying partner fee, linking AWS accounts, etc.

It was overwhelming and out of my access level and I parked that for later, as I knew the main focus area was to get people certified. So I decided to come back to the task list later.

## The Plan
Next, I documented the different AWS certifications level, what those are, their prerequisites, their difficulty level, their exam cost, who should take which one, how many are needed in each level to be in the Select tier.

I explained to Anand that these certifications require a fair bit of committed preparation and that usually takes a few weeks to months. I presented the plan to Anand and we discussed that people would be motivated to take the exam and stick to taking the exam if Technogise would reimburse their exam cost. Anand readily agreed and was even committed to getting people retake the exams should they fail but he was concerned about people giving up or taking months to take their exam, citing project commitments.

This was a genuine concern. While being AWS certified was beneficial for both the employee and the company, the company had vested interest to get its people certified sooner to be able to make to the Select tier.

This was a challenge I knew would eventually show up and for which I had no definitive answer. For one thing, this was about getting things done by others that I had no control over. It was different than leading a team where I had more control over staying "within the timelines". But owning for the outcomes of someone else's commitment was something that I feared.

I had thought more than once if I chose the right initiative to deliver for the company. The only thing I had was my own experience of passing the exam a few years back and that I was confident to inspire others. But if others would really go past the finish line or if my experience would relate to them was a mental block that I feared quietly.

## The Pause
I did not commit anything to Anand at the time, but acknowledged his perspective. I felt I should talk to fellow interested colleagues first, assess genuine interest.

I asked for Anand's permission to announce formally about the program to the company to attract interested colleagues to sign up to take the exam. Having taken the exam myself and preempting the kind of questions that would come up from an employee's perspective, I prepared a FAQs document which not only answered the most important questions for the program but also the financial support from Technogise and how to claim it. I went over the FAQs a few times until I was satisfied with it if I were an exam taker.

Finally, I sent an announcement to the company and invited interested people for an open call.

The call went really well. People came with tons of questions — which is always a good sign. They were keen to know about the certification paths and about my own experience as well. What I noticed that when I talked about my own journey, they felt more engaged, so I shared the ups and downs of my own journey with them, how I felt demotivated to continue at times and how I kept at it. I also noticed that there was a sense of relief when they got to know that Technogise was going to support their effort financially.

I set an expectation that preparation without a target exam date wasn't the goal — pick a cert, start preparing, and set a date. My own timeline of about 2-3 months of preparing and taking the exam after that helped them with a clear perspective.

## The Feedback Loop Continues
I went back to Anand with people's feedback and set expectations with Anand about timelines. I told him that while the organisation's expectations are communicated, I asked Anand to keep realistic expectations that it'll be about 3-4 months before we can see a few certified people in the company.

## The Learning Platform
I created a Slack channel with all the participants so that we could keep sharing how things are moving with each other and exchange experiences to keep everyone engaged. The channel was kept public to encourage more people to hop on and kept the FAQs and other study material, Udemy shared accounts, etc. bookmarked to the channel for an easier onboarding.

I knew the AWS exam content is substantial and it is hard to keep focus for 1-2 months and study daily, with project commitments. So every week, I kept sharing my approach on how to stay disciplined for an extended duration without losing the required curiosity. I also set timeline reminders on the channel to nudge them, conducted 1-1 with those who needed specific support, and even guided genuine people to take a pause and rejoin when ready. What was pleasing is to see a few participants create groups so they could prepare together.

I gave them a few rules of thumb to follow:
- The goal is not to finish the preparation by 2 months, the goal is to study for 1 hour daily.
- Avoid compensating if you miss to study for a day, as it can get boring and overwhelming if one has to set aside 2 or 3 hours in a day due to compensation.

Once we had 3 people sign up for their exams, we just kept nudging each other for progress and help. This went on for 1-2 months.

## Partner Scorecard
While I wasn't directly pairing with the participants in their preparation and I had my own project commitments, I got an hour here and there in a week to explore how to get their exams recognised on partner central. Again, put on my research hat and went through the Partner Central portal, found some blogs written by other AWS partners on the steps to get the certification counted on the portal. The portal had a scorecard that displayed red and green against the requirements. I had my own certification still valid from the past so I followed the steps and got my certification populated on the scorecard.

## AWS Certified
Finally, after 3 months, we had our first 3 colleagues AWS certified!

I kept sending org-wide reminders of the program at intervals and more people signed up.

I repeated the cycle of going through FAQs with new people and have them pair up with certified people to exchange experiences.

By this time, a clear FAQs doc, skill builder guide, what to do before and after taking the exam, how to reimburse, when to reimburse — everything got refined and ready to scale.

What started with tons of unknown now felt like a process in place.

## Other Requirements
While all this was happening, I noticed that there was no traction on the other requirements — accreditations, launched opportunities and MRRM. I kept asking Anand if Vivek got a chance to explore these as he had signed up to explore this area initially. Anand mentioned that Vivek may not have the time to explore, as he was busy with some sales activities.

I took it upon me and found how to get accredited via Skill Builder. However, due to lack of clear documentation and blogs online, I was not sure though if I was on the right path so I took the necessary training myself first and observed if it showed up on partner central. Once it did, I understood the process and created a guide for others to follow to take the necessary trainings for accreditation.

I assigned one each training to Anand, Vivek Singh and Vivek Veeramani to meet our requirements.

By now, two out of the 4 requirements were clear.

The requirements for launched opportunities and MRRM were not straightforward and there were no guides available. I understood that these two were something that had to be tried out on the portal and I alone did not have all the information needed to fill the forms.

This is when I asked Anand to have someone else — someone from sales — get more involved and experiment with me, as I could not get further by myself. Anand volunteered and I asked for a pairing session with him. We went ahead and submitted one entry. We still had a lot of questions, so I suggested to leave one entry and wait, instead of entering the required 3. Once it gets counted or we get queries from AWS, we'd know more.

Eventually, we learnt about the process of AWS approving the entries and updating our partner scorecard.

We then submitted all 3 required entries with MRRM.

By now, all 4 requirements were clear:

1. AWS Accredited Individuals :white_check_mark:
2. AWS Certified Individuals :white_check_mark:
3. Launched Opportunities :white_check_mark:
4. Monthly Recurring Revenue Minimum (MRRM) :white_check_mark:

Now, we fully understood how certified people counted on the portal, how to get people accredited and how to showcase opportunities and MRRM.

## The Task List
It was now that I went back to the portal task list that I had parked as it was overwhelming back then and started looking at it closely and figured that I did not have the knowledge on many of them and I will need another set of hands. I started assigning Anand those tasks in the portal that really needed his inputs to execute them, paying the needed partner fee being one of them.

Anand also responded positively and explored those tasks proactively and together we were able to close most of the tasks.

## Outcome
8 months since the first company-wide announcement, we got our AWS Select Tier Partner badge and we could explore everything that AWS had to offer us in that tier.

## Takeaways

This was a great experience for me and I learned that my fear to lead the program was genuine. I wanted to lead an org-wide problem end-to-end and the ups and downs in the journey tested me.

I knew I was good at keeping teams motivated, and I had only heard that getting things done by people who are not directly working with you but on who you depend is really hard. The program made me relate to the Directors and CTOs of a company. But my approach of leading teams — the human aspect, clear communications and expectations, timely feedbacks, and managing roadblocks came in handy and I was proud that those same techniques worked out at org level too.

My natural curiosity was put to test when I had to provide answers to lots of unknowns to people who themselves do not have the time to do it. I wasn't an expert but being comfortable with not knowing, and going to find out, turned out to be enough.

I also learnt that if people's concerns are addressed head-on without filters and when they do not have fear of failure, they're more likely to succeed.

I knew some people may drop out, some may get demotivated during the journey and slip timelines — and it happened, yes. But this was something I kept with myself and did not tell Anand who may panic. I knew I just had to get enough people to pass the exam, so I kept my focus on that.

I learnt about managing people beyond team, clear communication that impacts the whole company, the timing of communication, what to radiate, what to hold back, to ensure the right people stay motivated, the leadership stay informed, while maintaining transparency on both the sides.

I learnt to validate the path myself before putting others on it. Whether it was getting certified, completing accreditation trainings, or submitting the first launched opportunity — I went first. That made me credible, and ensured others' time wasn't wasted.

I learnt that I was able to deliver an org-level initiative while being on a full-time project.
