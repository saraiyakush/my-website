Which behaviour are you celebrating and is it good your culture?

Imagine you are an engineering leader and you have two teams who build products for your customers. Both the teams work "consistently", but there is a pattern to their consistency.

Team A is usually seen attending at least 2-3 incidents in a month to put off some fire that's happening in their products. They join the incident and the fire is put out in a instant. Until the team joins the incident, others have no idea what happened or how to fix the problem. It is only after someone from Team A intervens that it seems like things are moving and that the problem that's been burning for a while can be put off. If an incident occurs for Team A's products, at least one person from the team has to put aside everything that they're doing to put out the fire.

Team B is hardly seen attending incidents. They are almost never called into incident investigation. Heck they do not even know an incident happened or is happening until after it was resolved. Team B never stops doing what they're doing because they're never called to any incidents.

## Achievers of the Week

When the time comes to gather the names of people (or teams) who are the achievers of the week, guess which team's individuals are recognised? Team A or Team B?

Team B will hardly make the cut.

Why?
Because incidents are visible. They are tangible, they can be counted, they can be quantifiable. The **efforts** in the incident are visible. The people who attend incidents are visible. The people who fix the problem are visible.

Because Team A really went out of their way to fix a problem that our customers were facing.

Team A receives a huge round of applause and every other week, you'd see Team A showing up on the leaderboard.

What does Team B do?
They need to justify why they never make the "achievers" cut. Because they pay the price of quietly preventing the fire in the first place. Because they address smells that could lead to a fire before the fire even happens. Why is that?

Because prevention is hardly visible. No one knows the decision Team B took 4 months ago that did not result in an outage in their products while Team A was fighting valiantly last week.

Because Team B hardly ever have to change their timelines to accommodate for ad-hoc incidents because they delivered a comprehensive support documentation for support teams that even when the incident occurs, the support teams never have to call them and the incident never turns into a fire that needs to be out off.

And that's expected. How can you reward something that is not visible? You can never be in their heads at all times nor you can expect the team to publish every micro decision they take that prevents some pattern.

## Consequences

Team A are promoted. Team B silently leave the company. You feel Team A is more hard working than Team B because you have _seen_ it.

## Is this the right way to recognise teams?

Think twice. When your incentive structure is solely based on visible outcomes, it can easily be gamified. Create dependencies, become irreplaceable, be a hero on the day something breaks. Cash in on the accollades. Repeat.

Such people are always thin sliced because they need to be everywhere. They can hardly deliver planned tasks because they're constantly pulled into last minute emergencies. They can never take on more work because they're running behind on earlier tasks. They simply do not have time to think or innovate.

Whereas Team B can ensure they stick to the plan. They bring predictability to their work, their documentations create smaller psuedo teams who can independently carry out tasks. They embrace sharing knowledge so that they can be easily moved to something else. They have the time to experiment and innovate.

Which team would you want in your company for long run?

## How to incentivise?

Flip the metrics.

Measure the following for the products that teams deliver:
- No. of incidents in the past year. (Lower, the better)
- No. of support teams needed for each incident. (Lower, the better)
- Time taken to resolve each incident. (Lower, the better)
- No. of days worked for more than 8 hours in the past year. (Lower, the better)
- Times when a fix was already being applied before an incident was opened (Higher, the better)

## Symptons of teams who do this

Such teams are extremely good at planning, they account for unknowns, highlight risks, say "no" to you when they cannot meet the timelines, put up their team for success. They foresee risks and create mitigation plans to address them, effectively eliminating the risk. They're aware of a problem before your customer faces it.
